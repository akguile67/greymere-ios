# Dictation spike: read-aloud script

For the spike in `dictation-spike-plan.md`. **Test text only.** The sentences use the names of people and places
from the Route cast and settings, but the events are made up for the test and are not canon.

Read it at your normal dictation pace and distance, in a quiet room, **without slowing down or spelling out the
names**; the point is to see how each route copes with how you really speak. About 365 words: roughly three
minutes. When you reach the last paragraph, carry on talking freely for about two minutes about the book and its
people, using the names naturally. Record all of it as one Voice Memo.

## Read this (clean copy)

Morning. I'm going to read this the way I would dictate it, at my normal pace, without slowing down for the names.

Leah Tran drove out to Alderton Bay before it was light, with the heater on and her notes on the passenger seat. She had three questions for DCI Arthur Calder, and she wrote the hardest one on her hand so she would not lose her nerve.

At Sunset View the gate was open. Besnik Bajraktari was standing by the office with his phone in his hand, talking quickly in Albanian. She did not catch all of it, but she caught two names: Dritan Hysaj and, a moment later, Sokol Bajraktari. Then he saw her and put the phone away.

Later she sat in the car and wrote it down. Fatos and Erion had been at the caravan park the week before. Lindita Hysaj had stopped coming to the shop. Sofia Hysaj was not at school, and her friend Klea would not say why.

Far from there, in Văleni, in the Iza Valley in Maramureș, Mia Florescu was helping her sister Ana Florescu with her homework. Nobody in the village had asked about Marta Florea in years, and nobody wanted to start.

On the coast, in Sarandë, Marjeta was arranging flowers for the charity dinner. She was kind to everyone who came through the door, and she remembered every name. Her husband, Sokol Bajraktari, was upstairs on the telephone. Their son Luan Bajraktari would be flying in from Tirana on Thursday, by way of Durrës, and then on to Shkodër at the weekend.

By the evening Leah Tran had written four pages for The Greymere Chronicle. She read them back, took out two paragraphs, and put one of them back in. Her editor wanted to run it on Friday. She said she would need until Monday.

Dara Voss rang at nine and said only, "Don't use my name." Leah Tran said she wouldn't. She did not say what she would do about Besnik Bajraktari, because she had not decided.

That is the end of the read-aloud section. Now I will talk freely for about two minutes about the book and the people in it.
