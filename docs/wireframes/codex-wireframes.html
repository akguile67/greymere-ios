Codex panel — wireframes for iOS 0.9.0

Sprint 1, step 2 of ADR 0.59.0 (platform card 0.59.0). Targets: iPad mini 7 (landscape 1133×744 pt, portrait 744×1133 pt) and iPhone 14 Plus (428×926 pt). Sample content is invented. Decisions already made by the author are built in: no World tab; objects are cards; locations are cards with Contains chips; search by name and alias is the primary way in; appears-in-chapters and tappable body references are out; every edit is a proposal shown as a diff, applied by the author, and refused if the entry changed meanwhile; image generation, Standardise, Godric context, Find missing and Ask Godric are in. Orange R marks a "needs a mobile rethink" item from the parity checklist; the table at the end maps every R to its treatment and to the screen that shows it. The clickable version of this navigation is the throwaway prototype (CodexPrototype/).

1. The navigation in one paragraph

Search first. The Codex opens on a search field. Typing matches name and alias (and body), across every entry including rooms inside buildings. With nothing typed, the screen shows what you were last reading, then Browse by type (counts include everything), then the tools (Find missing, Ask Godric, series scope). Browse by type is one tap away: on iPhone, a row under the search field; on iPad, a chip strip above the list. iPad: always two panes (list left, entry right), in landscape and in portrait (the list is narrower in portrait but never hidden, so there is no sidebar toggle to find). iPhone: drill-down stack, home → list → entry, with back swipe. Contains chips on a building move you down into a room; the back chevron brings you back up. Everything that edits (edit, standardise, image, delete) lives behind one ⋯ menu on the entry, so the reading screen stays quiet. Godric context is a single toggle pill on the entry ("Godric is looking at this").

Why this shape

The reading screen has no tabs and no permanent toolbar of ten buttons. The two things you do most away from the desk — read, and ask Godric about this entry — sit in the bottom pair; everything that changes data hides in ⋯.

Contains chips are the only in-body navigation (tappable references in bodies are out). A chip pushes one level; back returns. The "In: Harrow Hall" chip on a room is the same link going up, so a room reached from search is never a dead end.

Objects use the same card and the same reading screen as characters; there is simply no map. An object's "Where it sits" is just text in its body.

The one edit path

Typed edit, dictated edit, Standardise, an accepted image, Godric's own change: all of them end on screen G. One review screen, one Apply, one refusal (H). That is the only way a write reaches the Codex from the phone.

On refusal nothing is lost: the proposal stays, the current text is shown beside it, and "Re-propose on latest" re-runs it against the new version.

R Series, books, scope and Appears-in are edited in the same flow, so they also appear in the diff as chips added or removed.

R The hypothesis was "portrait collapses to the phone pattern". Wireframe proposal instead: keep two panes in portrait (list ≈ 280 pt, entry ≈ 460 pt). Reason: a drill-down on a 744 pt-wide screen wastes most of the width and loses the list you were scanning. The prototype lets the author compare both (see its Settings switch "iPad portrait: split / drill-down").

Where this panel lives

Opened from the app's floating glass menu workflow tray like Planning, never a tab bar of its own. The ⋯ at the top right of the list holds: New entry, Series scope, Find missing, Ask Godric, Show contained places.

10. Things the prototype is meant to answer

1) Is search-first right at several hundred entries, or does the author reach for Browse by type more? 2) Do Contains chips and the "In: …" chip make a building/room hop feel safe, or does the author get lost two levels down? 3) iPad portrait: split or drill-down? 4) Is one ⋯ menu for every write too hidden? 5) Does the scope control belong on the home screen or in a settings row?
